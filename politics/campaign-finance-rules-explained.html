Campaign Finance Rules: A No-Nonsense Guide

Campaign finance rules are the playbook for how political money moves. They set who can give, how much, and what gets reported. Here’s the straight story.

5 min read Politics

The Basics

Campaign finance rules govern money raised and spent to influence elections. They cover contributions, expenditures, and disclosure. The goal is transparency and preventing corruption. Rules exist at federal, state, and local levels. Federal races follow the Federal Election Campaign Act (FECA). The Federal Election Commission (FEC) enforces it. States have their own versions. Local rules vary widely.

Who Can Give and How Much

Individuals can give directly to candidates, but limits apply. Those limits are adjusted for inflation. Political action committees (PACs) can also give, with different caps. Corporations and unions cannot give directly to federal candidates. They can form PACs. Super PACs can raise unlimited sums but cannot coordinate with candidates. For example, if the individual limit is $2,900 per election, a donor can give that amount to a candidate for the primary and again for the general. Check current FEC figures for exact numbers.

The Disclosure Game

Disclosure is the backbone. Campaigns must report contributions and spending. Reports are public. The FEC posts them online. Donors above a certain threshold must be itemized. That threshold is $200 for federal races. Below that, donations can be unitemized. Disclosure lets voters see who funds campaigns. It also helps catch violations.

Enforcement: The FEC and Beyond

The FEC is a six-member commission. By law, no more than three members can be from the same party. That structure can lead to gridlock. The FEC handles complaints and audits. But enforcement can be slow. The Department of Justice handles criminal violations. States have their own enforcement bodies. In recent years, some have criticized the FEC for being too lenient. Others say it’s overreach. The debate continues.

Why It Matters

Rules shape who can run and who can influence. They affect trust in the system. Loopholes exist. For instance, dark money groups don’t have to disclose donors. That’s legal but controversial. Understanding the rules helps you follow the money. It also helps you spot when someone bends the rules. Stay informed. Check official sources like the FEC website for the latest.
